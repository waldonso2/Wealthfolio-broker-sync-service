# Features in Detail

Reconciliation, X-Ray, the booking model, and everyday operations. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## Reconciliation with Wealthfolio

After every sync, the service compares broker data with Wealthfolio: cash balance against the cash account (except for comdirect, where the current account is not booked), verifies that the securities account holds no cash (it must be zero) and that neither account contains a `$CASH` position, and— for Trade Republic, Scalable Capital, comdirect, and Deutsche Bank—compares every position with the broker's portfolio holdings. The *Broker* page shows the result. If a discrepancy persists through the next sync, you receive an ntfy notification. Wealthfolio may need a moment to recalculate after new bookings. Common causes are corporate actions that need manual booking, or holdings from before the configured start date; add these as opening positions on *Review*.

**Services** (the home page) lets you enable or disable each service that writes to Wealthfolio independently. The gear opens that service's page (`/services/<service>`); all service pages follow the same layout: name, toggle, and what it writes to Wealthfolio (if something is missing before it can be enabled, it is shown with a link); current status (last run, what was written, what is missing and why—no amounts); settings; and actions. *Run now* starts the same step that runs after a sync, in the background. Where supported, *Remove from Wealthfolio* asks for confirmation, removes only data written by this service, and disables the service. Broker Sync shows a status light and last sync for each broker, the sync mode (change it under *Security*), and a link to *Set up broker*.

The **Review** page also shows imported bookings that have disappeared from Wealthfolio (*Re-create* or *Ignore*), transactions the broker no longer reports, residual cash in securities accounts, and transfers without a matching entry.

## X-Ray, concentration risks, and FIRE

The **X-Ray** page in the main menu shows the stocks held inside your ETFs and how they combine with your directly held stocks, across all Wealthfolio accounts:

- **Overview:** each stock appears once, with its euro value inside each ETF (one column for each of the four largest ETFs, the rest combined under “Other ETFs”), direct holdings, total value and portfolio share. It initially shows the top 10. Anything not represented in holdings lists (cash, derivatives) is shown as “unallocated”; no extrapolation is performed.
- Choose to show the top 10, 25, 50, 100, or all stocks.
- Each card (Overview, Overlap, and individual ETFs) is collapsible, and each ETF inside a card can be expanded. An expanded ETF initially shows its ten largest holdings; click to load the full list only when needed, keeping large ETFs fast. Each ETF links to its page at Finanzfluss (finanzfluss.de, opened by your browser; the service sends nothing there).
- **Per ETF:** the 25 largest holdings, their ETF weights and your euro exposure (country is shown on wide screens), with an option to expand to all holdings. The card also shows the holdings date and source, plus *Fetch now*. If a holdings list is unavailable, the reason is shown (provider unavailable, ISIN unknown to provider, no automatic download, no ISIN in Wealthfolio, etc.). For iShares, you can enter the product page under *ETF settings*. An ETF without a holdings list does not appear as a row in the overview table, which lists stocks only; the page indicates which ETFs are missing. Synthetic (swap-based) ETFs are marked: their list represents the fund's securities basket, not the index.
- Individual stocks and ETFs from other providers are currently hidden; they count as one position each in the portfolio total.
- Collapsed sections below include **Concentration risks** (a stock over 5%, a country over 60%, a sector over 30%, foreign currency over 50%, two ETFs with over 50% overlap, fewer than 30 effective holdings, more than €100,000 cash in one account, or a total expense ratio over 0.5% per year; new alerts are sent once via ntfy and *Intended* dismisses one), **Costs**, **ETF overlap**, and **Settings** for concentration thresholds.

The service fetches ETF holdings lists **directly from providers**, without requiring setup, every morning during the sync—even in secure sync mode, in which no brokers are contacted. It identifies providers from ETF names in Wealthfolio and funds by ISIN. **iShares:** first uses a bundled list of all iShares funds and product IDs (updated weekly and shipped with each service update), then searches country-specific iShares fund finders. If a fund is not found, enter its product page once using *Search iShares*; the service downloads the file behind “Download data” from www.blackrock.com. **Xtrackers** (etf.dws.com) and **SPDR** (www.ssga.com, via the fund finder and ticker) provide Excel files; **Amundi** (www.amundietf.de) and **Invesco** (dng-api.invesco.com; the index list is preferred so swap ETFs show index constituents) provide lists by ISIN; **Vanguard** (www.vanguard.co.uk), **VanEck** (www.vaneck.com), and **ARK** (europe.ark-funds.com, including Rize ETFs) provide holdings via product pages; **HANetf** (hanetf.com) provides a fund holdings file. A bundled, weekly refreshed list maps HANetf ISINs to funds; if an ISIN is missing, the service queries the provider's fund list once per sync. **L&G:** enter the holdings-list URL once under *ETF settings* (the “Fundholdings.csv” download on the ETF page at fundcentres.lgim.com); the service then fetches it daily. WisdomTree and UBS block automated downloads, and there is no open way to obtain complete holdings for HSBC and some others. X-Ray explains this and lets you upload the ETF holdings file from the provider (CSV or Excel, including UBS `.xls`); the uploaded list remains in use until replaced. *Fetch now* on an ETF refreshes its list immediately; the button at the top refreshes all ETFs, with progress shown. A provider (and Finanzfluss for TER data) receives only the ISIN of funds you hold. Not all holdings lists include ISINs: the same stock across ETFs is matched by ISIN, or by a normalised name (removing legal form and nominal value); small or unusual securities may therefore appear twice.

The **Costs** card estimates costs from purchase to sale, per position and for the portfolio: order fees and taxes from Wealthfolio bookings, spread, ongoing costs (TER), and custody fees for the holding period since the first purchase, plus an estimate of today's sale costs. It calculates the weighted TER, annual total expense ratio, and annual costs in euros. Taxes are shown separately and do not count toward the expense ratio. TER is fetched alongside holdings lists from iShares and SPDR fund finders, Amundi's product API, ARK fund pages, and HANetf's product list. For other ETFs (Xtrackers, Invesco, Vanguard, VanEck, UBS, etc., including uploaded lists), the service reads TER from the ETF's page at Finanzfluss (www.finanzfluss.de; only the ISIN is sent there; refreshed every 30 days). A TER entered under *ETF settings* always takes precedence. Without a TER, a position has no expense ratio; the portfolio figure reports how much is missing. Enter spread, sale fees (fixed and/or percentage), and custody fees per broker under *Broker fees*; if no values are configured, the service shows “not estimated”. Ongoing value is the average of cost basis and current value, so it is an approximation.

Optionally, the service can also write ETF **countries and sectors into Wealthfolio** (the *Countries & Sectors* service on the *Services* page). Wealthfolio's own analytics and target allocations then look through ETFs instead of assigning a single country to each ETF. Only automatic values sourced from Yahoo are replaced; custom assignments are preserved. Country names are matched in English and German (e.g. “United States” / “Vereinigte Staaten von Amerika”) and by country code (“US”). Sectors are also matched in German and English, including industry names (e.g. “Banks” maps to Financials). iShares holdings lists do not include countries: for broad funds, the service uses iShares' own country breakdown; for single-country funds (MSCI India, S&P 500, etc.), it uses the country from the iShares fund finder; otherwise it uses the country another ETF reports for the same company. If an ETF's holdings list contains country or sector data for less than half the fund (L&G, uploaded lists, etc.), the service falls back to Finanzfluss's country/industry breakdown for the whole fund (refreshed every 30 days; only the ISIN is sent there). The ETF card indicates when data comes from Finanzfluss. Remaining holdings (cash, “Other”) appear as “Unknown” in Wealthfolio. The service page explains missing assignments and the source of country/sector data per fund. *Remove from Wealthfolio* removes all values written by this service and disables it.

**X-Ray inside Wealthfolio:** The *X-Ray in Wealthfolio* service (on *Services*) stores the holdings lists in Wealthfolio after each sync for the X-Ray add-on. The add-on displays X-Ray directly inside Wealthfolio and makes no network requests itself. Only provider holdings lists and fund names/ISINs are stored—no amounts. Wealthfolio 3.11 or newer is required. The service page shows when data was last stored, which funds have lists and why others do not, links to the add-on (install in Wealthfolio via *Settings → Add-ons → Install from file*), and provides *Store now* and *Remove from Wealthfolio*.

The **FIRE calculator** (link at the bottom of X-Ray) estimates the portfolio needed to fund a lifestyle in today's money under German rules: capital gains tax plus solidarity surcharge and optional church tax, applied only to the gain portion of a sale; 30% partial tax exemption for equity funds; saver allowance; health and long-term care insurance as a voluntarily insured member of statutory insurance (or a private premium; minimum contribution only in years without pension or part-time income, and no contribution once compulsory pensioner insurance applies); and statutory pension from its start date. It shows Lean, Regular and Fat FIRE (75%, 100%, 150% of spending), Barista FIRE with part-time income until pension age, when the target is reached, and the portfolio level at which Coast FIRE applies. Assets and gain share come from Wealthfolio; costs come from X-Ray and are deducted from returns, with a comparison showing results without costs. All values can be overridden. This is a simplified model using 2026 values, not tax or investment advice.

## Booking model

- **Two accounts per broker:** buys, sells and dividends go to the securities account; everything else goes to the cash account. Cash for a purchase, sale proceeds and dividend cash move between accounts as transfers (`TRANSFER_OUT`/`TRANSFER_IN`, linked by `sourceGroupId`), so the securities account does not retain cash. Wealthfolio does not count these transfers as expenses. For comdirect, a purchase is funded by a deposit to the cash account and sale proceeds are withdrawn from it because the current account is not imported.
- **Fees and taxes** are stored in their dedicated booking fields. A dividend is booked at its net amount with tax in a separate field. A tax refund is a separate credit (`CREDIT/TAX_REFUND`).
- **No duplicates:**
  1. The service remembers every imported transaction.
  2. Wealthfolio rejects exact duplicate bookings itself.
  3. Before creating a booking, the service checks whether it already exists, for example from a CSV or PDF import: same account, type and security; no more than 36 hours apart; same quantity; and amount within ±0.02.

  If a run stops halfway through, the next run completes the missing steps.
- Each booking includes a comment like `[SYNC <broker>:<id>]`. The service uses it to recognise its own bookings; do not edit these comments.
- **Backfilling tax:** if an imported dividend lacks tax (Scalable's CSV export omits it), the service adds the tax from broker data, changing only the tax field.

## Everyday use

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Push notification “Login required” | Tap it, enter the TAN, and you're done |
| Push notification “Sync failed” | Open *Broker*; the reason is shown there. Often the next run succeeds automatically |
| “Unknown transactions” | The service does not recognise a transaction type and has **not** imported it. *Unknown transactions* shows details (IBANs and account/portfolio numbers are hidden). Use *Complete and book* to classify it as buy, sell, position in/out without payment, split or reverse split (new shares per old share; 1:10 = 0.1), and add missing ISIN, quantity or amount. The next sync books it once like any other transaction. You can undo the classification before then. You can also open an issue with the transaction type so the service can support it natively |
| Reconciliation shows cash in the securities account or too much/little in the cash account | Open *Review*. It lists times when cash remains in the securities account (e.g. a purchase without a transfer or a transfer whose sale was deleted) and transfers without a matching entry, including date, amount, comment and whether the booking came from the service or an import. Make corrections in Wealthfolio; this page does not modify data |
| Re-import a broker from scratch (e.g. after rules changed) | In broker settings, choose *Reset…*. The page shows how many service-created bookings would be deleted; deletion happens only after you type the broker name. The next sync re-imports everything from the start date. Imports and manual entries remain |
| Transfers to your own account (e.g. savings at another bank) | On *Transfers*, add a rule using an IBAN or keyword. Matching withdrawals become transfers instead of expenses. Once rules exist, the page appears under *Settings*, where you can also delete them |
| Change the UI password or sign out everywhere | *Settings → Security → UI password*. Changing it ends all other sessions; *End all other sessions* does the same without changing the password. A session lasts at most 12 hours and expires after 30 minutes of inactivity. Common passwords such as “password1234” are rejected. Changing credentials, resetting a broker, or changing sync mode asks for the password again if the last authentication was more than 15 minutes ago |
| Push notification “Mismatch with Wealthfolio” | A previously imported booking is missing in Wealthfolio (perhaps deleted), or a service booking is no longer reported by the broker (e.g. reversed). Open *Review*: use *Re-create* for missing bookings or *Ignore* if they should remain absent; delete reversed bookings from Wealthfolio |
| Use a ticker rather than an ISIN in Wealthfolio | Under *Securities*, map the ISIN to the desired symbol |
| Analyse account expenses in Wealthfolio | On *Services*, open the gear for *Expense tracker* and select the cash accounts (one list for all brokers). After the next sync, the cash accounts appear in Wealthfolio's expense tracker (the account must have type Cash). Wealthfolio assigns categories using its rules under *Expenses → Rules*, based on payee and description; transactions are not changed. Transfers and securities settlements count as savings, not expenses. When disabled, the service removes the accounts unless you had selected them manually in Wealthfolio |
| Securities in Wealthfolio without an ISIN | Nothing to do: after each sync, the service fills in missing ISINs for known securities. Wealthfolio can then identify imports, prices and bonds, and find logos. Other security fields are preserved; an existing ISIN is never overwritten, and an ISIN you removed is not re-added. The *Complete ISINs* page shows what was added and when, and which securities still lack an ISIN; use *Missing ISIN? Assign manually* if needed |
| Securities in Wealthfolio without logos | On *Services*, open the gear for *Logos*, enter a (free) logo.dev key and enable it. After each sync, the service fetches logos by ISIN and sets them in Wealthfolio (3.8.0+). Only ISINs are sent to logo.dev; custom logos are preserved and a removed logo is not re-added |
| Prefer English, a different font, or a fixed light/dark mode | *Settings → Appearance*: font (same as Wealthfolio, Sans, Serif, Mono), theme (Light, Dark, System), and language (Deutsch, English). The icon at the top flips the colours until logout |
| Check version, links, and outbound data destinations | *Settings → About* (also linked at the bottom of every page) |
| Switch light/dark mode | The UI follows the device by default. The half-circle icon at top right toggles the appearance on all pages until logout |
| Forgot the UI password | In the Proxmox container console, run `brokersync-reset-password`. It displays a new setup code; use it on the next visit to set a new password |
| “Unknown host name for this UI” | The UI responds only to its IP addresses, container name (including `.fritz.box`, `.lan`, `.local`, `.home.arpa`), certificate names and the address configured for notifications. This prevents another website reaching it via its own name (DNS rebinding). The IP address always works. Add another name with `Environment=BROKERSYNC_ALLOWED_HOSTS=name1,name2` using `systemctl edit wealthfolio-broker-sync` |
| Times in a different time zone | Set `Environment=BROKERSYNC_TZ=Europe/Vienna` (or another zone) with `systemctl edit wealthfolio-broker-sync`. English UI displays dates as `2026-03-15` and amounts as `1,234.56` |
| UI behind a reverse proxy | Otherwise, every request appears to come from the proxy and five wrong passwords could lock everyone out. Add the proxy address as `Environment=BROKERSYNC_TRUST_PROXY=<IP>` in a drop-in for `wealthfolio-broker-sync.service` (`systemctl edit wealthfolio-broker-sync`); rate limits then apply per user |
| JSON logs (e.g. for a central log system) | Set `Environment=BROKERSYNC_LOG_FORMAT=json` with `systemctl edit wealthfolio-broker-sync` (and `…-run`); each message is a redacted JSON line |
| Prometheus metrics | `https://<address>:8443/metrics` shows runs per broker and result, plus last-run time, duration and success, after login. For a scraper, set `Environment=BROKERSYNC_METRICS_TOKEN=<at least 32 characters>` and use `Authorization: Bearer <token>`. Amounts and names are not exposed |
| Push notification “Broker Sync locked” (after restart when a master passphrase is configured) | Tap the notification and enter the passphrase |

## Known limitations

- **Without SQLCipher:** SQLCipher is available for x86-64 and ARM64. On other platforms (e.g. 32-bit ARM), the sync database in `data/` is not encrypted. Credentials and settings remain encrypted; the *Security* page indicates this.
- **Without a TPM:** `systemd-creds` encrypts the service key using the container host secret (`/var/lib/systemd/credential.secret`); a container does not use a TPM. Someone copying the whole container, for example as a backup, can therefore access the data unless a master passphrase is configured. Where `systemd-creds` cannot encrypt, the key is root-readable only under `/etc/wealthfolio-broker-sync/`.
- **X-Ray:** Country and sector names are kept as providers report them (“USA” and “United States” are counted separately); currency-hedged ETFs still count as foreign currency; a company named differently by two providers may appear twice. Provider pages are not official APIs. If a provider changes its page, X-Ray reports the error for that ETF and counts it as one position until the service is updated.
- **Trade Republic:** There is no official API. The service uses the web app's interface via pytr; if Trade Republic changes it, syncing may stop until an update.
